Summary

The work connected two disciplines: deciding which product change would help users, and directing agents through a controlled engineering process.

This case study brings together PULSE's onboarding iteration and my broader AI assisted delivery practice. The onboarding result demonstrates product judgment. The engineering workflow describes how I used specifications, tests, and review gates across product work.

Delivery workflow

  1. User signal and product decision
  2. Specification and implementation
  3. Testing and engineering review
  4. Release and production feedback

User Signal and Product Decision

I used product instrumentation, funnel analysis, A/B tests, community feedback, and user research to identify friction in the game. For onboarding, the outcome that mattered was whether a first time user completed their first lineup.

The resulting changes moved registration until after initial play, replaced the tutorial with a milestone system, and adjusted the pace and speed of actions. This let users experience the game before committing to registration and gave them guidance through progress.

Engineering Workflow

Across PULSE development, I used agents to turn product ideas into specifications, tests, implementation steps, debugging loops, and validation tasks. My responsibility covered the architecture, database, frontend, backend, and production operation.

01

Define the change

I framed the user problem and product objective. Agents helped develop specifications and implementation steps.

02

Direct implementation

I directed coding agents against specifications, retaining responsibility for the architecture and the changes accepted into the product.

03

Test and review

Implementation passed through test and review gates. Agents also supported debugging and validation work.

04

Operate and learn

I owned releases, monitoring, production support, and defect investigation. User feedback and analytics informed subsequent work.

This is a summary of my delivery practice, not a reproduction of an individual ticket, pull request, or release log.

Verification and Ownership

Engineering verification and product validation answer different questions. Testing and review address whether the implementation behaves as intended. Production observation addresses whether that behavior helps users reach the intended outcome.

I used agents within both the development and investigation workflow, but remained responsible for reviewing their work and operating the resulting system. At PULSE, that responsibility included daily releases, monitoring, defect investigation, data recovery, and user support.

Engineering principle

A clear specification gives an agent a target. Tests and review provide evidence about its implementation. Production feedback shows whether the original product decision was useful.

Outcome and Limits

The onboarding changes increased the share of first time users completing their first lineup by 236%.

This is the reported relative improvement from the combined onboarding changes. It is not a percentage point increase, a result attributed to AI alone, or a measured gain in engineering speed. The public case study does not include the underlying cohort sizes or measurement window.

The lesson is to keep responsibility connected across the whole process: identify the user problem, define the change, review its implementation, and evaluate what happens after release. Agent assistance belongs inside that process.
